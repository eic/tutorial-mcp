## Priya — graduate student starting an ePIC analysis

Priya is a second-year PhD student who has just joined the ePIC collaboration. She is comfortable
on the command line and has written some Python and a little ROOT, but she has never used an AI
coding assistant and is sceptical that one can be trusted with physics. She wants to reconstruct a
V0 (Λ⁰ → p π⁻) without first spending a month learning the full Key4hep/PODIO stack. This lesson
shows her how to drive a free assistant through an MCP tool server to get a verifiable invariant-mass
peak, and — just as importantly — how to check that the result is real.

## Marco — postdoc who wants a reproducible, shareable workflow

Marco already scripts his analyses and uses an AI assistant occasionally, but his prompts and
conventions live in his head, so his runs are hard to reproduce and impossible to hand to a student.
He wants durable project context (`AGENTS.md`), a versioned procedure (`SKILL.md`) with explicit
success criteria, and a setup that behaves the same whichever assistant a collaborator happens to
run. This lesson gives him the "standards in the centre, tools at the edges" pattern and a worked
example he can adapt to his own measurement.

## Dr. Chen — group leader evaluating AI tooling for the collaboration

Dr. Chen is deciding whether to invest in shared AI infrastructure for her group. She is less
interested in any single assistant than in whether the approach is portable, auditable, and free of
vendor lock-in. The lesson's emphasis on an open protocol (MCP), verification against ground truth,
and the EIC MCP-server catalogue helps her judge what belongs centrally and what stays local.
